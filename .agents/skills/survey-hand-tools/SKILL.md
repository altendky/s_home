# Survey Hand Tools

Answer the user's actual hand-tool question proportionally. A narrow question
does not require a market survey or preliminary direction check. When used with
`survey`, provide category expertise while the coordinator owns scope,
candidate reconciliation, and the final comparison.

## Define The Tool Function

Describe the required action without assuming a tool style. Establish what the
tool acts on, the direction and magnitude of force, required precision or
control, access and clearance, frequency and duty cycle, workpiece damage risk,
and consequences of slipping or failure.

Distinguish tools that look similar but perform different roles, including:

- driving and turning;
- gripping, holding, clamping, and crimping;
- cutting, stripping, scraping, filing, and shaping;
- striking, prying, pulling, and separating;
- retaining-ring, clip, hose, seal, and other specialty-service operations;
  and
- manually operated layout, checking, and adjustment tools.

Treat calibrated torque, dimensional, and diagnostic instruments as precision
tools whose calibration, traceability, service, and range position require
special attention. Do not generalize ordinary hand-tool quality criteria to
them without adjustment.

## Choose Meaningful Comparison Dimensions

Adapt the analysis to the tool rather than ranking brands generically. Relevant
dimensions may include:

- working-end geometry, contact area, fit, alignment, edge or tip formation,
  and workpiece damage risk;
- size, reach, angle, offset, swing arc, head thickness, joint location, and
  clearance;
- material, heat treatment, hardness where documented, rigidity, toughness,
  wear surfaces, finish, corrosion resistance, and replaceable components;
- joint, ratchet, drive, hinge, adjustment, locking, or return-spring design;
- leverage, handle shape, grip, hand clearance, fatigue, and controllability;
- capacity, useful working range, accuracy, repeatability, calibration, and
  overload behavior where applicable;
- serviceability, consumables, replacement parts, warranty path, and expected
  life; and
- complementary tools, adapters, or setup steps required to complete the job.

For a specific application, identify which geometry and construction details
matter, then let `survey-application-fit` classify whether the evidence proves
the match.

## Use Category-Appropriate Evidence

Prefer manufacturer catalogs, drawings, manuals, standards, and product-
specific support terms; then authorized specialist distributors, controlled
tests, technical demonstrations, and detailed exact-application reports.

Use relevant ASME, ISO, DIN, IEC, or JIS definitions and performance standards
when they materially clarify interfaces or claims. For vehicle-specific work,
use applicable manufacturer service information for the procedure, special-
tool designation, dimensions, torque values, and safety requirements.

Classify consequential claims as **Documented**, **Corroborated**, **Inferred**,
or **Unknown**. Do not treat alloy labels, country of company ownership,
marketing tiers, finish quality, set size, tooth count, maximum capacity, or
visual similarity as proof of overall quality or common manufacture.

## Check Common Hand-Tool Traps

- Confirm that nominal size, profile, drive, tip, jaw, pin, or blade geometry
  matches the actual interface.
- Distinguish maximum advertised capacity from a controllable useful range.
- Account for head thickness, handle sweep, access path, nearby obstructions,
  and the room required to engage and release the tool.
- Do not assume an adapter preserves strength, accuracy, clearance, or warranty.
- Distinguish impact-rated tools from superficially similar hand-use tools.
- Compare equivalent package contents and required complementary pieces rather
  than set count or headline price alone.
- Treat apparent private-label relationships as hypotheses unless documented.

## Give Basic Use And Care Guidance Safely

Explain tool-specific setup, engagement, force direction, inspection, and care
when relevant, without inventing a repair procedure. Do not recommend striking,
extending, grinding, heating, electrically insulating, or otherwise modifying a
tool unless its manufacturer permits that use. Do not assume a covered handle
is voltage-rated. Call out stored-energy, pulling, spring, sharp-edge, pinch,
impact, and workpiece-ejection hazards when the tool or application makes them
material.

Keep vehicle-specific repair sequences, wear limits, fastener identification,
torque specifications, and diagnostic procedures with authoritative vehicle
service guidance.

## Return The Right Shape

For standalone questions, answer only as broadly as needed and identify any
application fact that controls the answer. For a full survey, return:

- the functional tool taxonomy and meaningful mechanisms;
- category-specific comparison dimensions;
- geometry, access, construction, service, and safety considerations;
- useful authoritative sources and standards;
- common misleading comparisons; and
- the least costly reliable validation for unresolved fit or performance.

Do not launch a full market survey unless the user asks for one.
